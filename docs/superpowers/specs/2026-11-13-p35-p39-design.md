## P35 — A turnover hazard as a per-frame switch cost (P23 I, IOHMM-lite)

**Belief.**
- A change of team costs `switch_team` 6 nats on every frame: in a dribble, a duel, a pass in flight, or 30 s into a
  quiet spell.
- The turnovers of DFL's flag concentrate where the features P32 already computes say a hand-over is under way:
  - one team's control has just ended and the other's is about to begin (`since_episode_s`, `until_episode_s` of both
    teams);
  - the ball turns or accelerates (`ball_turn_*`, `ball_acc_*`);
  - it moves towards the other goal (`ball_vel_adv`);
  - one team's advance changes sign (shape v2's one-sided columns).
- A classifier of "a direct change of team within ±0.5 s", trained on DFL, gives a per-frame probability h_t. The
  decoder then charges less for a change where h_t is above its base rate and more where it is below.
- This differs from what the emissions already do. They say *who* has the ball, smoothed over windows of 2 to 10 s.
  The hazard says *where* a change happens and reads the ball's event signals, which no team source uses for timing.
- It is the one mechanism here that can lower merges (raw) and splits (coarse) at once, because a global switch cost
  only trades one for the other.

**Falsified if** the hazard does not separate the frames near a change from the rest (the gate), or the decoder gains
nothing from it (the bar).

**Why scale 1 is the principled value.** In an IOHMM the cost of a change at t is −log of its per-frame probability.
DFL's 336 direct changes a match over about 79,000 alive frames give 0.43% a frame, which is −log 5.45 nats: close to
the chosen `switch_team` 6. So `switch_team − (log h_t − log h̄)` is the input-dependent transition at the decoder's own
calibrated base cost. The emissions are over-counted (the posterior is 4 to 6 times too sharp, A25), so the right
scale in the decoder's units may be above 1. Hence the candidates 0.5, 1 and 2.

**Mechanism.** The interface is declared here, so that A1 and A2 build in parallel.

*A1, the decoder* (opus; `pipeline/decode/hmm.py`, its README section, `pipeline/selftest.py` after the hmm checks):
- `decode(ctx, ..., hazard_source=None, hazard_scale=None)`.
  - `hazard_source` names one of the block's evidence sources. `hazard_scale` is a finite number ≥ 0 (None means 1.0
    with a source).
  - Refused: `hazard_scale` given without a source; a name that is not a source of the block; a negative or
    non-finite scale; the combination with `restart_source`.
- The named source contributes **nothing** to the emissions, neither its `home` nor its `alive`
  (`log_likelihoods(..., skip=name)`). Its `home` is the hazard's z_t (below).
- For each frame t ≥ 1, `team_cost[t] = clip(switch_team − hazard_scale · logit(z_t), HAZARD_FLOOR, 2 · switch_team)`,
  with z clipped to [eps, 1 − eps]. A NaN z_t gives `switch_team`. `HAZARD_FLOOR = 1.0` nat, a module constant and
  not a knob.
- `viterbi(..., team_cost=None)` and `_best_path(..., team_cost=None)`. With None, the loop is exactly today's. With
  an array, the H ↔ A predecessor of a run entered at frame t costs `team_cost[t]`, and the D changes cost
  `switch_dead` as before. The tie order is unchanged.
- `marginals(..., team_cost=None)`: the H ↔ A entries of `link` per frame. The forward birth at t uses
  `team_cost[t]`; the backward leave towards t + 1 uses `team_cost[t + 1]`. This is needed for `diagnose.py` on a pick.
- `diagnose.py`: `posterior_parameters` passes the hazard to `marginals`, and the calibration block leaves out the named
  hazard source's `home` (it is not a team probability).
- Checks:
  - every existing hmm check unchanged;
  - a hazard source that is NaN everywhere, and `hazard_scale` 0, both give labels identical to no hazard;
  - brute force over every valid path of tiny blocks with random per-frame costs, for `viterbi` and for `marginals`;
  - a flat-evidence stretch between two teams' runs with a hazard peak at frame k: the change lands on k, not on the
    stretch's first frame;
  - the refusals above;
  - 80,000 frames within 1.5 times today's time.

*A2, the model* (sonnet):
- `pipeline/benchmark/train_turnover.py`, cloned from `train_team.py` (profile, seeds, folds, `generic` without a
  Calibration, `--dry-run`, peak memory):
  - **Rows:** every 5th graded frame whose raw truth is H or A.
  - **Label:** 1 when a *direct* change of team in the raw truth (H ↔ A, no `D` between: codes[c − 1] and codes[c] both
    in {H, A} and different) lies at |t − c| ≤ 12 frames in the same graded block; 0 otherwise.
  - **Features:** `team_features.features(inputs)` (72).
  - **Mirrors:** the mirror rows are `team_features.swap(X)` with the *same* label (a change is a change whichever team
    is which).
  - **Fit:** `train_dead_ball.HGB_SETTINGS`, one model per held-out match and `full`, each storing its rows'
    `base_rate`.
  - **Artifact:** `pipeline/models/turnover_hgb.json`: `format`, `kind` "hgb", `target` "team_change_pm12",
    `window_frames` 12, `feature_version` 2 (`team_features`'), `features`, and the folds and `full`, each with
    `base_rate`.
  - **`--report`:** pooled and per-match held-out AUC, log-loss and the base-rate log-loss, and a GO / NO GO line.
    Printed beside it, gating nothing: the AUC with the ball seen and in the gaps, and the localisation (for each true
    direct change, the offset of z's maximum within ±25 frames: median, quartiles, share within ±5).
- `pipeline/evidence/turnover_hazard.py`, with `compute(run, *, model, min_prob=0.001)`:
  - the model is `folds[inputs.game]` if the file has that fold, else `full`;
  - p = clip(sigmoid((r(x) + r(swap x)) / 2), min_prob, 1 − min_prob), which is exactly swap-invariant;
  - **z = p / (p + b)**, with b the model's own `base_rate`, so logit z = log(p / b);
  - `home` = z on graded frames with features, NaN elsewhere; `alive` NaN.
  - Registry line `"turnover_hazard": turnover_hazard.compute`.
  - Refused at config load: a `turnover_hazard` source that the decoder's `hazard_source` does not name. Unnamed, it
    would vote as a team source.
- Configs `pipeline/benchmark/configs/p35/`: `p35_s000.toml` (sanity: it must reproduce the reference to the last digit;
  not a candidate), `p35_s050.toml`, `p35_s100.toml` and `p35_s200.toml`. Each is the reference plus one
  `[[evidence]]` table (`kind = "turnover_hazard"`, `name = "hazard"`, `model = "pipeline/models/turnover_hgb.json"`)
  and `hazard_source = "hazard"`, `hazard_scale` = 0 / 0.5 / 1.0 / 2.0. A check pins them against the reference.
- Checks: the label on a synthetic truth (a direct change, a change through `D`, a change at the block's edge); the
  mirror keeps the label; z's symmetry under swap; the refusal of an unnamed hazard source.

**Candidates and pick.** Scales 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0. The pick is the highest mean of the two truths; among those within

C0's fresh seeds, the shift re-derived by R030's rule (reported, not applied) and the leave-one-match-out estimate.

Risks the benchmark cannot show:
- **T32:** ours has wrong-team slots on at least 10.8% of the graded frames and `generic` has none. An identity switch
  makes a team's columns jump, which looks like a hand-over, so the hazard may spike there and make spurious changes
  cheaper.
- **T38:** inside our gaps, `since_episode_s` and `until_episode_s` partly measure the gaps' timing, which on our match
  follows the play.

**Effort** about 2.5 engineer-hours: A1 45 to 60 minutes (opus: the tie rule and bit-for-bit are delicate), A2 45 to 60
minutes (sonnet), the reviews 30 minutes. Heavy about 20 minutes: main check 0.2, dry run 1, training about 10 (1.75

**Odds** 1 in 5. For it:
- input-dependent transitions are the best-supported structural idea in the decoding literature;
- ASRF's boundary branch, which refines frame labels by a separate boundary probability, is the same split, and is the
  standard remedy for over-segmentation;
- the clean-pair bucket is still the largest.

Against it:
- P30 already took the late tail off the starts;
- the remaining late tail is probably at `D` (C1 will say);
- the hazard reads features the emissions already use;
- scale 2 may add possessions, the grain.

**Rules check.** Trained on the seven DFL matches degraded with `generic`; the scale is chosen on them; the base rate
is a DFL training statistic stored in the file. On our match the fixed model reads our tracking at run time, which is
running, not fitting. Nothing of our match enters.

**Literature** (standard searches, abstracts only):
- [ASRF (Ishikawa et al., WACV 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06866) splits temporal action segmentation into
  frame-wise classification and **boundary regression**, and uses the boundary probabilities to refine the frame
  labels. Its abstract reports large gains against over-segmentation. That is P35's split: the team sources are the
  frame classifier, the hazard is the boundary branch.
- IOHMMs let transition probabilities depend on inputs (Bengio and Frasconi 1995; [hmmTMB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14139)
  and LaMa's inhomogeneous HMMs, cited in P23).
- Adams' segmented iHMM ([arXiv 1602.06349](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06349)) gates each transition with a
  segment-boundary probability, without covariates.
- Change points as the HMM's most likely state switches: Luong, Perduca and Nuel,
  [arXiv 1212.1778](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1778).
- In football:
  - [PathCRF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12080) detects on-ball events from players alone with a transition-constrained
    CRF;
  - [Vidal-Codina et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00804) assign possession and detect events from tracking with a
    tree;
  - an informal [CMU SURE project](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/cmsac/sure/2025/showcase/soccer_tracking/report.html)
    predicts forced turnovers from tracking with gradient boosting (a 23% class imbalance).
- No paper found tests a learned turnover hazard as an HMM's switch cost for possession.

## P37 — Touch timing in the gaps: what P32 sees where the ball is visible, carried into the gaps

**Belief.**
- In the gaps (41% of graded frames; headroom +0.180 raw / +0.104 coarse), the only team evidence is shape v2 at 0.3.
- P32's model gained where the ball is seen partly from each team's time since and until its last and next control
  (the hold class, 0.8991 against 0.8704).
- P29's table shows the same signal inside gaps. The agreement with the last controller is 92% at 1 to 2 s since the
  touch, then 82% at 3 to 5 s, 73% at 5 to 8 s, 62% at 8 to 12 s, and 47 to 55% beyond 12 s.
- Shape v2 is blind to it. A gap model that reads shape v2 *and* each team's since/until control can say who has the
  ball by time as well as by shape.
- That matters most in **handover gaps**, where the controller before the gap and the one after it differ. There the
  decoder must place a change, and only the gap's evidence says where.

**Falsified if** the held-out log-loss does not fall by the advisor's margin overall *and* in the handover gaps, or the
benchmark does not move.

**Why it is not P29's retry, nor a shape version 3.**
- It speaks only in the gaps, where neither the hold (NaN without a ball) nor P32 (ball seen only) votes. The hold
  region where the ball is seen is untouched.
- Its new columns are not shape. Its gate is the advisor's own figure for a chance at the bar (−0.013 of gap
  log-loss), and it adds a handover cell aimed at the non-redundant part (below).

**Mechanism.**
- `pipeline/evidence/shape_features.py`: `VERSIONS = (1, 2, 4)`. Version 3 stays retired, so that P33's file in history
  can never be read as this one.
  - Version 4 is version 2's 42 columns, copied (versions 1 and 2 bit for bit, as P33's code proved possible), plus per
    team, after its twenty, `since_episode_s` and `until_episode_s`.
  - Each is the seconds back to the team's last kept control frame in the block and forward to its next: 0 inside an
    episode, capped at 30 s (also where there is none), never across half time. Episodes come from
    `controlled_touch.compute(..., **TOUCH_RULE, confidence=0.99, hold=0.5)` as `team_features` builds them.
    `TOUCH_RULE` is frozen at `{"radius": 1.5, "max_rel_speed": 6.0, "min_frames": 1, "duel_radius": 1.0}`, and a
    check compares it with `team_features.TOUCH_RULE` and `main.toml`'s.
  - Team block 22, cross block 2 (`adv_gap`, `n_diff`), width 46, `_BLOCK_OF_WIDTH[46] = 22`. `swap` trades the team
    blocks, the new columns with them. There is no import cycle (`controlled_touch` imports neither shape module).
- `pipeline/evidence/shape_possession.py`: `SCOPES` gains `"seen"` (speaks only where the ball is present). The default
  `"gaps"` is unchanged.
- `pipeline/benchmark/train_shape.py`: `DEFAULT_MODEL_PATHS[4] = "pipeline/models/shape_possession_v4_hgb.json"`; the
  same rows, mirror and settings. `--report` gains, for every version, three cells of alive gap frames classified by
  the touch rule at `TOUCH_RULE`, independently of the model's columns: **handover** (the last kept controller before
  ≠ the next after), **same team**, and **unknown** (none on one side). Existing cells are unchanged.
- Configs `pipeline/benchmark/configs/p37/`:
  - `p37_split.toml` (sanity, not a candidate): the reference with the shape entry replaced by two, v2 at `"seen"` and

  - `p37_gap030.toml` (the candidate): the same, with `shape_gaps` reading the v4 file.
- Checks:
  - 46 unique names, and v2's columns equal in v4;
  - known since/until values on a synthetic touch scene;
  - capped and never NaN inside a block;
  - `swap` twice is the identity, and relabelled inputs give `swap(X4)`;
  - version 3 refused;
  - `"seen"` plus `"gaps"` equal to `"all"` in the emissions;
  - the handover cell on a synthetic scene.

**Step 0, the gate.** One chain: train v4, then `--report` on v4 and on v2. v2's existing cells must reproduce 0.3189.

advisor's figure), **and** (ii) on the handover gap frames v4's log-loss is at most v2's on the same frames − 0.013.
