## The approach

- **A learned team model beats a fixed rule** in every source that compares them: S9 by 7.3 points, and S8's learned
  model against its ball-and-distance heuristic. S1's rule is the reference, at F .83.
- **P19's middle form is sound:** per-frame boosted trees with temporal context in the features, decoded by our HMM.
  The literature's winners are sequence models (S7 to S9), and the HMM supplies the sequence here.
- **Left out as too complex:**
  - TCN, Bi-LSTM, set-transformer and CRF models: they need a GPU, carry a byte-for-byte reproduction risk, and are
    P20's lane.
  - Spearman's full pitch-control integral (PPCF, λ 4.3, κ, s 0.45) and Fernández and Bornn's Gaussian fields: both are
    fields over the pitch, and one minimum time to the ball is the per-frame summary a classifier needs.
  - S1's Bayesian network and trained thresholds: the trees learn the thresholds.
  - Per-player top speeds and Pleuler's direction penalty: slots have no identities (T15, T32).
  - An RTS smoother: `kinematics` already applies S2's SG(2, 7).
- **Labels.** DFL's `BallPossession` is the target, as frozen. S7 and S8 derive possessors from events, and S1's
  possession starts on entry to the action space. Possession conventions differ between sources, so the model learns
  DFL's, and the coarse truth stays the warning (T33).

## The frozen features, one by one

**Why the reach-time constants do not matter to the trees.** For a fixed reaction time r, the time to reach is
r + D_s / v, where D_s = \|p_s + r·v_s - b\|. Its minimum over a team's slots is r + min D_s / v, a strictly
increasing affine function of min D_s. `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` bins each feature by its quantiles, and quantiles
move with an affine map, so the bins and the trees are the same to floating-point rounding whatever v and the additive
r are. Only the extrapolation p + r·v carries information beyond the distance columns.

**Risk noted, no change.** DFL's tracking has rare jumps on a 1 cm grid, and the second difference behind
\|acceleration\| amplifies them. Our tracker is smoother (CLAUDE.md, "Data facts"). That is a train-to-match shift in
the ball-dynamics columns. The BAR-ATH transfer check (item 7) is the guard for it, so the design does not change.

## Proposed deviations (for the advisor; the plan implements the frozen design until told otherwise)

- **D1 (cosmetic, no expected effect on any score).** The frozen time to reach writes vmax as 7 m/s. S4, which the
  formula follows, uses vmax 5 m/s. Its 7 is the maximum acceleration in m/s², and its 0.7 s reaction is about
  vmax / amax = 5 / 7. By the argument above the choice cannot change the fitted trees beyond float rounding. Two ways
  to resolve it:
  - (a) keep 7 m/s and say in the docstring that it is not S4's value and does not matter;
  - (b) write 5 m/s, as S4 does.
  The plan implements (a) unless the advisor picks (b).

## Clarifications made in the plan (not deviations, listed so the advisor sees them)

- **C1.** "Present slots" per team are P07's `h_n` and `a_n`, already among the 26 shape columns. The team block does
  not repeat them, since two identical columns add nothing to trees. Only `over11` is new.
- **C2.** "Whole-rule LOMO (`choose.py`)" over a two-stage grid. Stage 2 exists only at Stage 1's full-data choice, so
  a fold cannot re-run both stages (R013 said the same). The bar's LOMO is `choose.py --reference main` over the union
  of the 16 distinct candidates of both stages, in the declared tie order. A fold may then pick any of them, which covers
  the selection across both stages. Each stage's own LOMO is reported beside it.
