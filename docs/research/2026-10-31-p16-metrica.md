## 2. Metrica's event taxonomy (`documentation/events-definitions.pdf`, 7 pages)

**Possession, Metrica's own definition** (page 1), verbatim:

> Possession: a player is regarded as being in possession as soon as they touch the ball or make a conscious decision
> to hold off on collecting the ball when they have the chance to, with the intention of taking control of the ball in
> a different position.
> The player continues to be in possession until one of the following takes place:
> - Another player takes over possession
> - A shot is deflected off the goal's woodwork or another player
> - The ball is significantly deflected by the referee
> - The ball goes out of play
>
> Possession is not considered lost when an opposing player's interference does not have a significant impact, i.e.
> when the opponent manages to get a touch in, but the attempted play still reaches its intended target or the player
> originally in possession retains control of the ball.

> Out of bounds: the ball has crossed the outer lines of the field.
> Out of play: the ball went out of bounds or the referee has stopped play.

The definition is touch-based ("as soon as they touch"), with a significance exception. That puts it between DFL's
every-touch end and StatsBomb's established-control rule (§4).

**Event types** (page 2 and 3), each with its subtype groups:

| type | subtype groups | definition (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| SET PIECE | Set piece, FK attempt, Retaken | "The player brings the ball back into play via a Set piece." |
| RECOVERY | Interference 1/2 | "The player significantly interferes with the opponent's possession, or brings the ball back into play via a dead-ball situation that is not a SET PIECE." |
| PASS | Body part, Attempt, Deflection, Offside | "Possession of the ball changes between two players of the same team." |
| BALL LOST | Body part, Attempt, Interference 1/Intervention, Deflection, Offside | "The player loses possession of the ball to an opponent or through interference from the referee." |
| BALL OUT | Body part, Attempt, Intervention, Deflection, Offside, Own goal | "The player is the last one to touch the ball before it goes out of bounds." |
| SHOT | Body part, Deflection, Shot direction, Shot result, Offside | "The player attempts a shot towards the goal." |
| FAULT RECEIVED | none | "A fault is committed on the player in possession and the referee immediately awards a free kick." |
| CHALLENGE | Challenge, Fault, Challenge result | "Two or more players engage in a challenge for ball possession or with the intent to disrupt one another." |
| CARD | Card | "The player is awarded a card by the referee or the referee dismisses someone from the technical area personnel." |

**Subtypes that matter for H/A/D** (pages 3 to 7):
- *Set piece:* FREE KICK, CORNER KICK, THROW IN, PENALTY, KICK OFF. **GOAL KICK is not a Set piece subtype.** It sits
  in *Attempt* ("The goalkeeper brings the ball back into play after it had gone out of bounds"), so a goal kick is
  tagged as a PASS, BALL LOST or BALL OUT with subtype GOAL KICK, not as a SET PIECE.
- *Interference 1* (on RECOVERY; "the opposite applies to BALL LOST events"): INTERCEPTION and THEFT ("The subsequent
  event is irrelevant (i.e. the ball may go out of bounds)"). *Interference 2:* BLOCKED ("they may continue to keep
  possession") and SAVED.
- *Intervention:* VOLUNTARY (gives the ball up for medical attention); FORCED ("Play is halted by the referee. This also
  includes any situation where the referee lets play continue after a fault has been committed (but decides to award a
  free kick at a later point) …"); END HALF.
- *Offside:* OFFSIDE ("The receiving player is flagged for offside or a free kick is awarded for offside while the pass
  is still underway").
- *Shot result:* GOAL, OUT, BLOCKED, SAVED. *Own goal:* GOAL.
- *Challenge:* TACKLE, DRIBBLE, GROUND, AERIAL. *Fault:* FAULT ("The referee awards a free kick as a result of a
  Challenge"), ADVANTAGE. *Challenge result:* WON, LOST.

**What the document does not say:** what an event's start and end frames mark. A BALL OUT's end could be the frame
where the ball crosses the line, or something else. The PDF has no CARRY type, although game 3's JSON has one
(kloppy, §3). Nor does the PDF say whether the two CSV games and the JSON game were tagged under the same version of
the definitions.

**The CSV format, from code that reads it** (Friends of Tracking, Laurie Shaw,
[Tutorial1_GettingStarted.py](https://github.com/Friends-of-Tracking-Data-FoTD/LaurieOnTracking/blob/master/Tutorial1_GettingStarted.py)
and [Metrica_IO.py](https://github.com/Friends-of-Tracking-Data-FoTD/LaurieOnTracking/blob/master/Metrica_IO.py)):
the columns `Team` (`Home`/`Away`), `Type`, `Subtype` (several subtypes joined by `-`: the tutorial finds goals with
`Subtype.str.contains('-GOAL')`), `Period`, `Start Frame`, `Start Time [s]`, `From`, `To`, `Start X`, `Start Y`,
`End X`, `End Y`. The tutorial does not use `End Frame` or `End Time [s]`. They are assumed by symmetry, and R023's
integrity checks test that assumption.

## 3. kloppy 3.18.0's Metrica loaders (our `.venv`, `kloppy/_providers/metrica.py`, `…/event/metrica/json_deserializer.py`)

- `metrica.load_tracking_csv(home_data, away_data)` reads games 1 and 2. `load_tracking_epts(meta_data, raw_data)`
  reads game 3. `load_open_data(match_id)` loads **tracking only**, from `metrica-sports/sample-data@master`, a branch
  and not a commit.
- `metrica.load_event(event_data, meta_data)` reads **JSON events only** (game 3). **kloppy 3.18.0 has no loader for
  the CSV events of games 1 and 2.**
- What the JSON loader does, and why R023 should not derive labels through it:
  - **SET PIECE events are dropped** ("set-piece events are integrated in the next pass or shot event"). They survive
    only as a qualifier on the next event, so the restart frame is lost.
  - Every event gets `ball_state=ALIVE`. A dead ball appears only as a synthetic `BallOut` event at the end time of a
    pass or shot whose result is out.
  - `ball_owning_team` is the event's team, except CHALLENGE and CARD (`None`).
  - Type 4 is built as `foul_committed`, under the event's own team. In Metrica's taxonomy that is the fouled player's
    team (FAULT RECEIVED).
- The type ids kloppy names in the JSON: SET PIECE 5, PASS 1, BALL LOST 7 ("INCOMPLETE"), BALL OUT 6 ("OUT"), SHOT 2,
  RECOVERY 3, type 4 (FAULT RECEIVED in the PDF), CARD 8, CHALLENGE 9, CARRY 10. The subtype ids: GOAL KICK 20,
  FREE KICK 32, CORNER KICK 33, THROW IN 34, KICK OFF 35, PENALTY 36, OFFSIDE 16, HEAD 11, WON 48, LOST 49, DRIBBLE 45,
  and the shot outcomes BLOCKED 25, SAVED 26, POST 27, OFF TARGET 29, GOAL 30. kloppy reads GOAL KICK (20) from the
  event's own subtypes and the other set-piece kinds from the *previous* SET PIECE event, which agrees with the PDF.
- **Consequence:** R023 should parse both formats with our own stdlib reader, as P01 did for DFL (T30), and use kloppy
  only to cross-check game 3's ids against the names.

## 4. Possession in the literature

**StatsBomb** (Open Data Events v4.0.0 and Open Data Specification v1.1, in
[statsbomb/open-data/doc](https://github.com/statsbomb/open-data/tree/master/doc)), verbatim:

> `possession`: Indicates the current unique possession in the game. A single possession denotes a period of play in
> which the ball is in play and a single team is in control of the ball.
>
> `possession_team`: The ID of the team that started this possession in control of the ball. Note that this will appear
> even on opposition events like tackles attempted during the possession.
>
> New possession are triggered after a team demonstrate they've established control of the ball. A new possession can
> begin even if the same team has possession of the ball for example, a blocked pass goes out for a throw in for the
> same team, this would be a new possession for the same attacking team.

This is a control-based rule: an opposition touch does not start a possession until that team has established
control. It sits at the coarse end of A06's literature range (175 to 220 a match). Like our scorer, it splits a
possession at every stoppage even when the same team restarts (`H H D D H H` is two possessions).

**PathCRF** (Kim, Lee, Seo, Ko, Yoon, Park, "PathCRF: Ball-Free Soccer Event Detection via Possession Path Inference
from Player Trajectories", [arXiv 2602.12080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12080), v1 12 Feb 2026, v2 1 Jun 2026;
CC BY 4.0 per arXiv's licence icon). The code is
[`applications/team_poss.py`](https://github.com/hyunsungkim-ds/pathcrf/blob/1b7c6f85a6b78c92e4543fde72ca2b86a933f997/applications/team_poss.py)
at `1b7c6f85`, under MPL-2.0. Its docstring for `compute_team_poss(events, tracking, mode)`:

> - mode="contested": gaps, episode boundaries, and team-change kicks are labeled "contested"
> - mode="split": gaps/edges assigned to event teams; team-change kicks are split half/half

In code, inside each in-play episode (`episode_id > 0`, from the tracking), with the events sorted by time:
- the head of the episode goes to the first event's team;
- between consecutive events of the same team, that team;
- between events of different teams, the gap is cut at `mid = (times[i] + times[i + 1]) / 2.0`, the first half to the
  earlier team and the second half to the later;
- the tail goes to the last event's team.

Every event of the other team flips the label. That is touch-based, with no significance exception, and the flip falls
at the midpoint. `compute_true_pred_poss` uses `mode="split"`. The paper's 92.4% (R022) is measured on this kind of
event-split possession on in-play frames, not on a flag.

**What the advisor's rule takes from each.** The rule changes team at RECOVERY, at BALL LOST, and at the first event of
the other team, with no midpoints. It is PathCRF's flip set, with the flip placed on the event's own frame rather than
at the midpoint. It is also Metrica's own definition: possession is held "until … another player takes over
possession", so the gap belongs to the earlier team. The midpoint would hand half of every gap to the team that has not
touched the ball yet. Metrica's significance exception is honoured only as far as the tagging already honours it: an
insignificant opposition touch produces no RECOVERY and no event of that team. StatsBomb's established-control rule is
not reproduced; it would need a control test the event data does not encode.

## 5. A06 and T33, checked against the advisor's numbers

- A06's grain statistics are **per alive minute** and **median length**, pooled over the seven DFL matches laid end to
  end (`pipeline/benchmark/truth.py`'s `summary`). Raw truth: **8.30 possessions per alive minute, median 3.68 s**
  (3,061 possessions, 55.2% alive, 668.4 graded minutes). Coarse truth (`COARSE_MIN_S` = 5 s): **4.50 per alive
  minute, median 8.40 s** (1,659 possessions, the same `D`).
- The advisor's **4.58 and 2.48 are per graded minute**: 3,061 / 668.4 and 1,659 / 668.4. They come from A10 and the
  benchmark tables (R002 on), not from A06. The two pairs have the same ratio (1.84). The per-graded-minute pair also
  carries DFL's alive share, and the sample games' alive share comes from our event-derived `D`. The draft therefore
  uses A06's per-alive-minute pair, as asked. The geometric midpoint is sqrt(8.30 x 4.50) = 6.11 per alive minute, and
  "within 15%" (symmetric in log, |ln(g / 6.11)| <= ln 1.15) is 5.31 to 7.03. For the median the midpoint is
  sqrt(3.68 x 8.40) = 5.56 s and the band is 4.83 to 6.39 s.
- A06 also warns that the rate per alive minute falls as the alive share rises (r = -0.71 over DFL's 5-minute windows).
  Whole matches span 48% to 59% alive, so the effect is smaller there, but a sample game whose derived alive share is
  far from DFL's would still be biased. R023 reports the alive share beside the rate.
- T33: rank everything that depends on possession length under both truths, and treat a disagreement as a warning; the
  coarse truth is a sensitivity check, not a correction. R023 is the measurement T33 asks for. Its outcome changes
  which truth is the objective; the other truth stays beside it.

## 6. Risks the draft names

1. **Construct gap.** Our GT comes from corrected-tracking flags. R023 measures event-tagged possession under
   Metrica's event definition and the advisor's derivation. Nothing measures how far an event-derived grain sits from a
   flag grain for the same provider. On DFL it could be measured: DFL publishes events for the same seven matches, and
   applying an analogous derivation there would calibrate the gap. That is a separate, unscheduled step for Tian.
2. **Start and end frames.** The PDF does not say what they mark. The draft uses the start frame where a team acts and
   the end frame where the ball arrives or goes out, and stops on integrity checks.
3. **Game 3 is a different format** (JSON, a CARRY type absent from the PDF) and possibly a later tagging version.
   It is reported separately as well as pooled.
4. **Small sample.** Three games give 6 halves. At DFL's raw rate that is about 1,300 possessions (437 a match), and
   at the coarse rate about 700 (237 a match). Either is enough for a pooled rate. The per-game spread is reported and
   not gated.
5. **P16's own text** says to add a truth of that grain if the grain lands between the two. The advisor's rule says to
   use both with equal weight. The draft follows the advisor, and Tian decides between the two.
